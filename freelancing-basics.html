Freelancing basics: turn a skill into a service

Freelancing means providing a service to a client, usually for an agreed fee. A clear offer and examples of your work are often more useful than claiming to be good at everything.

Pick one service

Examples include proofreading, short-form video editing, simple social media designs, transcription where permitted, research assistance, or website updates. Choose something you can practise and deliver reliably.

Create three samples

Make a small portfolio that shows the type of work you want to be paid for. Label practice projects honestly; never pretend a sample was made for a real client if it wasn't.

Describe the result

Instead of saying “I can do anything,” explain what the client receives, how long it takes, what information you need, and how revisions work.

Protect your time and money

  • Read the platform's current rules and fees.
  • Agree on scope, deadlines, and payment terms before starting.
  • Be wary of clients asking you to pay fees or buy gift cards to receive work.
  • Keep communication and payment on-platform when the platform requires it.

Freelancing can take time to establish. Keep improving your samples, ask for clear feedback, and track which outreach methods lead to genuine interest.
